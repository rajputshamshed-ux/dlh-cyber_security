                    MEDDEFENSE HEALTH SYSTEMS
                    BOARD SECURITY BRIEF - 30 JULY 2026
================================================================================

CURRENT THREAT STATUS:

A named ransomware group, Crimson Tide, is actively attacking U.S.
hospitals—five in ten days, three in our region, one (Hospital C) 45
miles from us in active containment tonight. They exploit the exact
FortiGate vulnerability present on our perimeter firewall and target
the exact weakness we have: unencrypted patient databases. We are in
the blast radius. We are taking emergency action.

SECURITY POSTURE VERDICT:

MedDefense currently has critical gaps across all five NIST security
functions. Our patient database of 50,000 records is unencrypted. Our
firewall has an unpatched critical vulnerability. Our network has no
segmentation. We have no intrusion detection. These gaps leave all
seven phases of the Crimson Tide attack chain exposed. This is not
acceptable—but it is fixable with the budget already approved.

EMERGENCY RESPONSE (EXECUTED IN LAST 12 HOURS):

The vulnerable firewall service has been disabled, closing the primary
entry point. Backup storage has been physically isolated to prevent
ransomware destruction. Enhanced logging is active on all critical
systems. A compromise assessment is underway to determine if we are
already breached. The permanent fix—firewall patching, database
encryption, and credential theft protection—is scheduled to complete
within 36 hours. We have engaged a 24/7 security monitoring service.

INVESTMENT SUMMARY:

The Board approved $120,000 for security in the July meeting. Of this,
$98,418 is allocated to emergency actions addressing this specific
threat. The FortiGate support contract renewal ($2,400) was approved
under the CISO's emergency authority and has been executed. Every
dollar of emergency spend returns approximately $285 in annualized
risk reduction. The remaining $21,582 covers the patient portal
certificate renewal and security awareness training. No additional
budget is requested at this time. FY2027 may require $180,000-$250,000
to sustain 24/7 monitoring and continuous vulnerability management.

RECOMMENDATION:

The security team requests the Board's acknowledgment of the emergency
actions taken and confirmation of support for the 72-hour plan. We are
not asking for more money. We are asking for your confidence that the
team is executing the right plan against a real and present threat.
The single most impactful action—database encryption—is in progress.
Once complete, the most catastrophic outcome (public exposure of 50,000
patient records) is neutralized because exfiltrated data will be
unreadable. We will report back to the Board within 72 hours with
confirmation that critical controls are operational.

Concern: Patient safety and organizational reputation.

"Dr. Morales, the direct patient safety concern here is clinical

the attack cannot succeed in the first place. On reputation: four

databases. We are encrypting ours within 36 hours. If we are
attacked after encryption is deployed, the stolen data is scrambled
and useless to the attacker—and to the press. We protect our patients
and our name in one action."

similar healthcare breach settlements. Third, on insurance: I need
you to verify immediately whether our cyber insurance policy covers

was unpatched. We meet both of those exclusion criteria right now.
We need to know our coverage status before we make any decisions
about ransom payment in the event of an attack. The encryption we
are deploying this week does not just protect data—it protects
MedDefense from the 'willful neglect' finding and may be required
to maintain insurance coverage."

ANTICIPATED QUESTIONS AND PREPARED ANSWERS
================================================================================

Q: "Why were we not protected already?"
A: "The Board approved the security budget four weeks ago. The controls
   that stop this attack are funded but in the deployment pipeline.
   The original timeline was a six-month phased rollout. The Crimson
   Tide campaign compresses that to 72 hours. The plan was right. The
   timeline has changed. We are adapting."

Q: "Should we pay the ransom if attacked before encryption is done?"
A: "That is a business decision for the Board, not a technical one.
   But I can tell you: if the database is encrypted before exfiltration,
   the double extortion threat is neutralized—the stolen data is useless.
   If attacked before encryption is complete, you face a $24.95M breach
   plus a ransom demand. Our strong recommendation is to complete
   encryption before that scenario materializes."

Q: "How do we know Hospital C's attack is the same group?"
A: "The CISA advisory attributes the campaign to Crimson Tide based on
   TTPs—tools, techniques, and procedures. The FortiOS exploit,
   Kerberoasting, unencrypted database targeting, and double extortion
   are specific to this group. Hospital C's profile matches."

Q: "What if we are already compromised and do not know it?"
A: "Our compromise assessment is running now. We are checking for
   unknown VPN accounts, new domain admin accounts, and unusual data
   transfers. If we find evidence of compromise, we shift from
   prevention to incident response. That is why we are doing this
   now, not waiting until morning."

Q: "Can we just shut everything down until this passes?"
A: "No. We are a hospital. Clinical operations cannot stop. Our
   approach is targeted: disable the specific vulnerable service, not
   the entire network. The IPsec VPN tunnels between sites remain
   operational. The patient portal remains accessible. We are
   surgically removing the risk, not shutting down the hospital."
