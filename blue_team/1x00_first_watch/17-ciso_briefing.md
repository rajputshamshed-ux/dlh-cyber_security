OPENING
-------
MedDefense has basic security but critical weaknesses that put patient
safety and our finances at risk. We are not prepared for a serious attack.

THE ONE THING THAT KEEPS ME UP AT NIGHT
---------------------------------------
Our MRI scanner runs on Windows XP - software that stopped receiving
security updates in 2014. It is connected to our main hospital network.
One attacker exploiting this vulnerability can move from the MRI to our
electronic health records, billing systems, and patient monitors.

Hospitals with this exact problem have lost $40 million and delayed
cancer treatments for a week.

WHAT WE MUST DO NOW
-------------------
1. Isolate the MRI and all medical devices on their own secure network.
   Cost: $22,000. Timeline: one week.

2. Require multi-factor authentication for all remote access and critical
   systems. Cost: $10,000. Timeline: one month. Phished passwords will
   no longer be enough.

3. Deploy threat detection and an incident response plan. Cost: $7,000.
   Timeline: one month. We will know immediately if an attack succeeds.

THE BUSINESS CASE
-----------------
The total cost for these seven priority fixes is $42,000 - less than
one percent of the $5 million recovery cost other hospitals have paid.
This is not an expense. It is insurance against catastrophe.

THE BOTTOM LINE
---------------
If we do not act, we accept the risk of a $40 million breach, patient harm,
regulatory fines, and lasting reputational damage. I recommend we approve
the $42,000 immediately.
